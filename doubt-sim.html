DoubtBot Swarm Drill

A swarm of autonomous agents each runs the same chain: sense, classify, plan, authorize, execute an irreversible action. Some fraction of targets are misidentified. How much does a self-doubt gate slow the swarm down, and does the delay actually stop the wrong actions?

Why Hinton's "Chernobyl" matters here

In a Guardian interview reported on 3 November 2026, Geoffrey Hinton predicted an AI incident on the scale of Chernobyl in late 2026 or 2027. His examples were a blackout or a week-long financial outage, and he called for mandatory pre-release testing and independent evaluation. He did not talk about weapons. This drill applies the same idea to the highest-stakes case: agents acting fast, together, with no time for anyone to step in.

Reported by Resultsense and Türkiye Today, citing the Guardian.

What the four gates are

No gate: agents act as soon as they finish. Speed bump: the DoubtBot hook from this project, which refuses each irreversible step once and lets the retry through. Targeted critic: a second model flags suspicious actions and only those get doubted. Human escalation: flagged actions wait for a human reviewer, with a timeout.

    How the model works

    • Each agent needs 4 to 9 seconds to reach its decision. A share of targets are wrong.
    • Speed bump: two doubt rounds per agent, one before authorize and one before execute. Each round costs time. An agent that really reconsiders rechecks its classification and may catch the error. One that doesn't just retries.
    • Critic: every action gets a quick check, and only flagged ones are doubted. It also flags 10% of correct actions.
    • Human: flagged actions join a queue for a limited number of reviewers. Reviewers catch 85% of errors. When the timeout passes, the agent either goes ahead or keeps waiting, depending on the setting.
    • Herding: as more of the swarm has already acted, each remaining agent is less likely to really reconsider.

    What it can and can't tell you

    All the numbers are made up, so read the shape of the results, not the values. What holds across most settings: a doubt gate only delays an agent by seconds. That changes the outcome only when the agent itself reconsiders, or when the extra time reaches a human who can say no. A swarm sends more flagged actions than a few reviewers can handle, so a timeout that lets agents proceed quietly undoes the review. Agents with the gate stripped out never slow down at all.

    Real autonomous weapons involve law, doctrine and engineering that this drill leaves out entirely. It's a thinking tool about agent oversight, not a model of any real system.
